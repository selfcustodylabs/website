Protecting your privacy

Protecting bitcoin privacy comes down to a few habits and three tools. The habits: never reuse an address, keep coins from different sources apart, do not post addresses or discuss your holdings, and think before each transaction about what it links. The tools: your own node, so no server learns your addresses; UTXO management and coin control, so payments do not tie your coins together; and CoinJoin or PayJoin, which break the links chain analysis relies on. Start with the habits and your own node, and add the rest as your threat model requires.

This page is the overview; why privacy matters and chain analysis explained cover the problem.

Which principles come first?​

  • Think before you act. Every transaction leaves a permanent trace, so ask whether it links your identity to an address, reveals more than it needs to, or could be done more privately.
  • Keep identities separate. Do not mix KYC bitcoin (bought with ID) with non-KYC bitcoin, work payments with personal ones, or public donation addresses with private savings.
  • Leak as little as possible. Use a new address for every payment, do not post addresses publicly unless you must, and consider who can see each transaction.

Why run your own node?​

A wallet that uses someone else's node or a public server reveals all your addresses, your transaction history and possibly your IP address to whoever runs it. With your own node the wallet's questions stay on your hardware, nobody else sees your addresses, and you verify everything yourself. What is a Bitcoin node and why run your own explain it, and the node guide sets one up.

Why manage your UTXOs?​

Your bitcoin is a set of separate coins, and how you spend them decides what observers can link. Combining coins from different sources ties them together, more coins mean higher fees, and careless spending reveals your activity. Label where every coin came from, never combine KYC and non-KYC coins, choose inputs with coin control, and consolidate only coins from the same source. The UTXO management guide covers it.

What does CoinJoin add?​

CoinJoin combines inputs from several people into equal outputs, which defeats the common-input-ownership heuristic and hides which output belongs to whom. After a good CoinJoin, analysts cannot tell which output is yours, and the coins' history is cut: they gain forward privacy. The mixed coins then need careful handling, and some exchanges flag CoinJoin history. The CoinJoin page covers both.

How can you acquire bitcoin more privately?​

Bitcoin bought on a KYC exchange is linked to your identity from the start, and no later technique fully undoes that. Other ways to acquire it:

MethodPrivacyTrade-offs
Peer to peerHigherMore effort, and a risk of scams
Some bitcoin ATMsMediumOften filmed, and many now require ID
Being paid in bitcoinHigherThe payer knows your address
MiningHighestExpensive equipment and technical knowledge

What about privacy at the network level?​

A transaction that looks private on chain can still leak your IP address when it is broadcast, when you come online, and who you connect to.

ToolWhat it does
TorHides your IP address
A VPNHides your IP address from everyone except the VPN provider
Your own nodeBroadcasts your transactions without a third-party server
Your own node over TorThe best combination

The Tor guide sets it up.

What can't be fixed afterwards?​

SituationWhy it cannot be undone
A withdrawal from a KYC exchangeThe exchange keeps your identity on record
An address posted onlineIt may be archived, scraped or saved
A payment to an identified addressThe transaction is permanent
Blockchain history in generalThe past cannot be changed

What you can do is start fresh with new coins, CoinJoin existing coins for forward privacy, and be more careful from now on.

What does each technique cost?​

TechniquePrivacy benefitCost or risk
Your own nodeNo address leakageDisk space and setup time
CoinJoinCuts transaction linksFees, time, and possible questions from exchanges
Buying peer to peerNo KYC linkA price premium and counterparty risk
Coin controlBetter privacy for each paymentManual effort and a learning curve

Where should you start?​

  1. Run your own node, with the node guide.
  2. Learn UTXO management.
  3. Use coin control on every payment.
  4. Consider CoinJoin for coins you already hold.

And keep the habits: a new address every time, labels on every incoming payment, sources kept apart, and a moment's thought before each transaction.
