Hardware setup for multisig

Before building a multisig wallet, each of its hardware wallets is set up on its own, as if it were a single-signature wallet: three devices, ideally from three manufacturers, each with current firmware, its own PIN and its own seed made with dice. For each one you record the master fingerprint, back the seed up on metal, store that backup somewhere different from the other two, and test it. Only then do the devices go into the Sparrow setup.

Step 1: Choose three devices from different makers​

Step 1 picks the devices. Mixing manufacturers means one vendor's firmware flaw, like the 2026 Coldcard entropy bug, reaches only one key, which a 2-of-3 wallet survives.

CombinationKey 1Key 2Key 3
Most isolatedJade Plus (QR)Keystone 3 Pro (QR)Trezor Safe 5 or 7 (USB)
Easiest to useTrezor Safe 5 (USB)BitBox02 Nova (USB)Blockstream Jade (USB or QR)
Lowest costBlockstream Jade (USB or QR)Trezor Safe 3 (USB)SeedSigner, built yourself (QR)

As of October 2026 three devices cost about $200 to $750 depending on the combination; the comparison has current prices. A Coldcard you already own can still serve as one key, after you check whether its seed needs replacing and make a new one with dice on fixed firmware; we just do not recommend buying one new.

Step 2: Check each device and update its firmware​

Step 2 makes sure each device is genuine and current before any seed exists on it. Buy from the manufacturer or a reseller it lists, check the packaging for tampering, run the maker's authenticity check, update to the latest firmware, and read the vendor's security advisories. A seed generated on flawed firmware stays weak after the firmware is updated, so update first.

Step 3: Create a separate seed on each device, with dice​

Step 3 gives every device its own key. Each device gets a fresh seed that has never existed anywhere else: never reuse a seed from another wallet, never put the same seed on two devices. Make each seed with dice: by hand, then restore it onto the device, or with the device's own dice entry where it has one (the Keystone 3 Pro and SeedSigner; 99 rolls for 24 words). Independent entropy for each key is what keeps one vendor's silent failure from mattering. Choose 24 words, and give each device its own PIN of at least 6 to 8 digits, with no obvious patterns and none reused between devices.

Step 4: Record each device​

Step 4 writes down what you will need to identify each key later. For every device, record:

FieldExample
LabelDevice 1
Make and modelJade Plus
Master fingerprint (8 characters)7c8a9b2d
Firmware versionAs shown on the device
Seed length24 words
Where the device is keptHome safe
Where its seed backup is keptBank safe deposit box

The master fingerprint identifies the key without revealing it; Sparrow shows it when the device connects, and it appears in the wallet descriptor. Store these records separately from the seed phrases, and label each device (1, 2, 3). The multisig record sheet has these fields for all three keys, plus the wallet details Sparrow gives you later.

Step 5: Back up each seed on metal, in separate places​

Step 5 makes each seed survive fire, water and time. Paper burns, gets wet, fades and gets thrown away by mistake, so stamp or engrave each seed onto a metal backup (Blockplate, Cryptosteel, Seedplate and Billfodl are common makes), read it back against the paper, and destroy the paper.

Then spread the backups out so that no single place holds two keys, and no seed is stored with its own device:

DeviceSeed backup
Key 1Home safeBank safe deposit box
Key 2Home, somewhere separate from key 1A trusted family member's home
Key 3A different city: a second property, an office or a trusted personA third location, such as a second deposit box

With this layout a burglary at home reaches devices but no seed; a lost deposit box costs one seed, and the other two keys still spend.

Step 6: Test each seed backup​

Step 6 proves each backup restores its device before the multisig depends on it. For each device in turn: reset it, restore it from its metal backup, and check the master fingerprint matches your record. The backup verification guide covers the method.

Before going on to Sparrow, check that all three devices have unique seeds backed up on metal in different places, recorded fingerprints, unique PINs, current firmware and labels.

How do you set up each device?​

The menus differ by maker. These notes cover what matters for multisig; follow the maker's own guide for the rest.

Trezor​

Connect by USB and set the device up in Trezor Suite. Trezor has no dice entry on the device as of October 2026, so choose to recover a wallet and enter the 24 words you made with dice, rather than letting it create one. Trezor PINs can be up to 50 digits. For multisig, Sparrow reads the key directly from the device over USB; no export file is needed.

BitBox02​

Connect by USB and set the wallet up in the BitBoxApp: restore the 24 words you made with dice, or follow BitBox's own diceware procedure, then set the device password. For multisig, Sparrow reads the key directly over USB and shows the fingerprint.

Jade and Jade Plus​

The Jade has no dice entry on the device: restore the 24 words you made with dice, or choose 23 words with Blockstream's dice method and let the Jade calculate the last, then choose QR mode for air-gapped use. For multisig, export the key to Sparrow by scanning the QR code the Jade displays.

Keystone​

Create the wallet on the device with a password and 24 words, using its dice roll option (firmware 1.2.6 or later). For multisig, export the extended public key from the device's multisig menu as a QR code or to a microSD card.

Coldcard (existing owners)​

Update first (Mk4 and Mk5 to 5.6.0 or later, Q to 1.5.0Q or later). Power the device from a battery pack or USB power adapter rather than a computer, set the prefix and main PIN, and create new seed words with the dice option (99 rolls), 24 words. For multisig, insert a microSD card and export the key from Advanced/Tools, Export Wallet, Generic JSON.
