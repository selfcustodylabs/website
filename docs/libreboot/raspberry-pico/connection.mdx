# Pico-Pomona clip connection

The Pico connects to the laptop's flash chip through the Pomona clip: six jumper wires run from the Pico to the clip, matching each chip pin to its Pico pin, and the clip then grips the chip on the motherboard. Two pins of the chip stay unconnected. The T530 and W530 need one extra part, which holds the second chip quiet while you work on the first.

## How do you find pin 1 on the chip?

The flash chips on these ThinkPads are SOIC-8 packages: eight legs, four on each side. Pin 1 is marked with a small dot or dimple on the chip's top. From pin 1, count anticlockwise around the chip: pins 1 to 4 down one side, 5 to 8 back up the other.

![Close-up of a SOIC-8 flash chip on a ThinkPad motherboard with its pins numbered 1 to 4 down one side and 5 to 8 up the other](/img/libreboot/soic8.webp)

The X230, T430, T440p, T530 and W530 have two chips side by side (4 MB and 8 MB), and both are flashed, one at a time. The T420 and T480s have one main chip. [Flashing](/docs/libreboot/flashing-bios/) covers each case.

## Which Pico pin goes to which chip pin?

| Chip pin | Function | Pico pin |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | CS (chip select) | 7 (GP5) |
| 2 | MISO (data out of the chip) | 6 (GP4) |
| 3 | WP (write protect) | Not connected |
| 4 | GND | 38 (GND) |
| 5 | MOSI (data into the chip) | 5 (GP3) |
| 6 | CLK (clock) | 4 (GP2, labelled SCK) |
| 7 | HOLD | Not connected |
| 8 | VCC | 36 (3V3) |

![Pin map of the Raspberry Pi Pico for serprog: SCK on pin 4, MOSI on pin 5, MISO on pin 6, CS on pin 7, GND on pin 38 and 3V3 on pin 36](/img/libreboot/serprog-pins.webp)

## What do the T530 and W530 need?

The T530 and W530 have two flash chips on the same SPI bus. They share the data and clock lines, and each has its own chip select (CS) line deciding which one is listening. With the laptop unpowered, the CS line of the chip you are not flashing floats: nothing holds it high or low, so that chip can switch on at random and talk over the one you are reading. The symptoms are read and write errors and dumps that differ every time you read the chip, which shows up as a different `sha1sum` on each read.

The fix is to hold the other chip's CS pin at 3.3 V, which keeps it switched off while you work on the first.

### What extra parts do you need?

- One more female-to-female jumper wire
- A test hook clip ([example](https://www.ebay.com.au/itm/165822939855))
- An AMS1117 3.3 V regulator module ([example](https://www.ebay.com.au/itm/134721404746))
- A plug-in power adapter with selectable output voltage, set to 5 V ([example](https://www.amazon.com.au/dp/B0C16PS3D7))

### How do you connect them?

1. Attach the extra jumper wire to the test hook.
2. Hook it onto the CS pin (pin 1) of the chip you are not flashing.
3. Connect the other end to the 3.3 V output of the AMS1117 module.
4. Connect a GND pin on the Pico to GND on the AMS1117, so the two share a ground.
5. Set the adapter to 5 V and plug it into the AMS1117's input. The AMS1117 is a regulator: it needs about 5 V in to produce a steady 3.3 V out.
6. Clip the Pomona onto the chip you are flashing, wired to the Pico as above.
7. Switch on the adapter. The AMS1117's red LED lights.

The inactive chip is now held off, reads match each other, and you can go on to [flashing](/docs/libreboot/flashing-bios/).
